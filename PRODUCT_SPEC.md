# GRVTY: Ride the Mountain

The strongest version is to make the app feel less like a habit tracker and more like a **team mission**.

For 10–12 year olds, the biggest motivational lever isn't self-improvement—it's belonging to something bigger than themselves. GRVTY already has a powerful identity that most youth teams lack.

App name: GRVTY: Ride the Mountain with the central narrative:

> We don't fight gravity.
>
> We ride it.
>
> Master it.
>
> Become one with it.

Every day, athletes prove they are becoming part of the Force.

---

# Core Idea

Instead of individual habit tracking:

**The entire team is climbing through the Mountain of GRVTY together.**

Each athlete contributes energy to the team.

The more habits completed:

* the stronger the athlete becomes
* the stronger the team becomes

The team succeeds only if everyone contributes.

This reinforces:

> One Team. One Force. For Glory.

# The Force Meter

The central game mechanic.

Every completed habit generates:

### GRVTY Force

Examples:

Core workout = +50 Force

Mobility routine = +40 Force

Homework = +30 Force

Healthy breakfast = +20 Force

Sleep goal reached = +60 Force

The team's total Force powers the expedition.

Instead of:

"Tom completed stretching."

Kids see:

> Tom added 40 Force to GRVTY.

This language is powerful.

# Team Cooperation Mechanic

This is where the concept becomes unique.

Every week the team receives a mission.

Example:

## Cross the Glacier

Need:

15,000 Force

Contribution:

Athlete A: 1,200

Athlete B: 900

Athlete C: 1,100

etc.

If completed:

Everyone wins.

Not just top performers.

This prevents weaker kids from giving up.

---

# The Rope Team Concept

Borrowed from mountaineering.

Every athlete belongs to a Rope Team of 4–6 athletes.

If one athlete disappears:

the rope slows.

If everyone contributes:

the rope advances faster.

Kids naturally encourage each other.

Not because coach says so.

Because they need each other.

**Do not make the app about tracking habits. Make the app about helping GRVTY complete a shared adventure where habits are the fuel.** That's what transforms routine behaviors into something a 10–12-year-old genuinely wants to participate in every day.
